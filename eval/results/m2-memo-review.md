## Findings, ranked by severity

### F1 (HIGH — B/A audit): The memo's load-bearing outcome number is quoted without its registered uncertainty bound, and the plan's own mandated phrasing is ignored

Memo, ledger (3923): "**Zero measured outcome-level benefit**: Q1/OUTCOME b = c = 0 at k = 12…" and Gap 1 (3930–3932): "found rank deltas of exactly this kind produced byte-identical `(file, symbol)` answers on 12/12 tasks."

The registered result attaches bounds the memo never states, anywhere:
- 2302–2303: "b + c = 0 at k = 12 → 95% upper bound on the outcome-changing rate = **22.1%** exact… **This is not equivalence.**"
- 2304–2305: "S-ident alone (n = 6…) → **≈39%**… both are reported, as registered."
- 2310–2322: "'The task set discriminated' is WITHDRAWN… three [of four failures] are ground-truth extraction artifacts… regraded, both marginals are 11/12 — **brushing the registered Gate 5 ceiling rule**, under which the set would be UNINFORMATIVE… effective discrimination is well below nominal k."
- 2330–2336: "the honest statement of what was shown is a notch weaker than that label, and **this is the version that should be quoted**" — followed by the block-quote the memo does not quote.

A concordance experiment whose own record says it could not have detected even a ~20% outcome-changing rate is being cited three times (ledger, Gap 1, DECISION paragraph 4012–4013: "the program's only outcome measurement says [the advantage] does not reach the user") as if it measured zero. This is exactly the program's rule "Report confidence intervals, not point estimates" (HANDOFF:290) being violated on the single most decision-bearing number, in the pro-delete direction. It does not flip the decision (the convergence argument survives), but the memo cannot ship without the 22.1%/39% bounds and the effective-discrimination caveat stated in the ledger itself.

### F2 (HIGH — B audit): The 147-call harness log is systematically promoted to "real usage"

Memo: Gap 1 (3933–3934) "**0 of 147** logged **real agent searches**"; Gap 4 (3981–3982) "that population is **the empirically dominant real-query shape** (the 147-search log)"; DECISION (4014) "the program's only **real-usage log**". Three problems, each verifiable:

1. **It is not a usage log.** The 147 calls came from the 30 Q1/OUTCOME harness runs (2373: "all 147 calls logged"), on a ~15k corpus, one prompt style, Bash surface, investigative read-only tasks — external-validity limits the registration itself records (2326–2327: "Bash surface rather than the MCP tool; `doc` chunks excluded in both arms; investigative read-only tasks, so code-change correctness is untested") and the memo drops. The program's actual real-usage instrument reports the truth: 4181–4186, "Q1-v2 HARVEST — re-checked 2026-08-02, still n=0 … organic=0 … POWER: have 0 / need ~67 -> INSUFFICIENT."
2. **What the log measured is narrower than what the memo cites it for.** The record supports "0 of 147 were the task text; rewritten into code-token shorthand" (2277, 4170–4171). It does *not* establish that queries carry **the target's declared symbol name** — the population DECLEX closure covers. "Identifier-bearing, median 5 words" is measured only on **harvest n=2** (4186, 2586–2588). Gate 4 of the same experiment found the target chunk in-window on only **3/12** tasks (2350–2352). Gap 4's "empirically dominant real-query shape" is therefore resting dominant-shape weight on n=2 while citing n=147.
3. **Generalization**: "agents rephrase" was observed for capable agents on tasks where rephrasing is possible. The memo's own Gap 1 concedes the residual ("a future query population that cannot rephrase"), which is correct — but then Gap 4 and the DECISION paragraph quietly re-inflate the log's authority.

Required change: everywhere, the log must be described as "147 harness-run agent searches (Q1/OUTCOME, ~15k corpus, one prompt)" and the dominant-shape claim re-attributed to harvest n=2 + the log's shorthand finding.

### F3 (HIGH — B/D audit): Gap 2's confrontation is partly circular and uses an inflated denominator

- **Circularity.** Memo 3959–3960: "**The bet:** on-target anchoring dominates real usage because real agent queries carry the target's own tokens (**S-ident fire rate 97.33%**)." The 97.33% (146/150, plan 3770–3771) is the fire rate on a *synthetic stratum constructed to contain the symbol name* (2585–2587: "query = symbol name + up to 3 rare content words from its TSDoc"). A stratum built to contain the anchor firing at ~100% is evidence about the generator, not about real queries. The only real-query support for the bet is harvest n=2.
- **Denominator inflation.** Memo 3953–3954: "The only observed off-stratum D effect in **3,200 original row-pairs** was one instance: rank 1→2, still in-window" (source: 3828–3830, accurate as far as it goes) — repeated as comfort in the bet summary (3999–4000: "its one observed instance moved a target one rank"). But D fired on 0.67%/0.50% of off-strata (3787–3788), i.e., exposure of order a handful of fires. Near-zero exposure ⇒ near-zero observed harm is *expected*, and one displacement among a handful of fires is a non-trivial per-fire harm rate, not a reassuring 1-in-3,200. The memo half-knows this (it correctly says the harm gate "structurally could not fire," 3946–3947) and then uses the row-pair denominator anyway. The bet may still be placeable — but on the mechanical-boundedness argument (3950–3952, which is sound) plus honest ignorance, not on these two supports.

### F4 (HIGH — E audit): The conditions and re-entry criteria are, as written, mostly theater

- **The harvest.** Condition 5 (4033) and re-entry criterion 1 (4041–4043) name the harvest "the standing real-query instrument." Its measured state: **n=0 organic since it was ready**, re-confirmed 2026-08-02 (4181–4186), with the recorded blocker "elapsed real usage of MAST for non-MAST work" (4194–4195) — a blocker no condition addresses. An instrument with no data source, no activation commitment, no threshold, and no review date is a fig leaf. To be real, the condition must commit to something falsifiable (dogfooding period, n target, or a dated re-entry review at which n=0 itself becomes a finding).
- **The kill-switch.** Condition 3 (4025–4026): "config flag, default on — the untested harm surface gets an operational escape hatch." Nothing measures the harm surface post-ship: the memo's own insulation thesis says ranking harm is invisible at outcome level (agents rephrase and recover), search telemetry is the very channel the harvest shows is not being populated, and the deleted `mode` discriminator removes the one existing signal. A switch nothing can ever trigger is not a safeguard. Cheap fix with teeth: require F18 to emit D-fire + pre/post-rank-displacement telemetry into the existing `metrics` table, so both the kill-switch and re-entry criterion 1 have an input.
- **Re-entry criterion 3** (4045): "Any outcome-level regression attributable to retrieval quality at scale" — attribution is impossible without an A/B, which criterion 4048 says will not be resurrected. Unfalsifiable as written.
- **Genuine parts, credited:** condition 5's off-repo retention of the embedded assets (real — re-entry avoids the 7.4 h embed), condition 2's regression fixtures (real, but they guard F18's *correctness*, not its *harm surface*), condition 4's total-deletion scope (consistent with the never-shipped record).

### F6 (MODERATE — D audit): The decision rule is applied asymmetrically, and "certain, large, permanent costs" overstates

- **Asymmetry.** The rule (3899–3902) admits retrieval-rank advantages "only insofar as a measured mechanism converts them" into paid units — applied to vectors. F18's entire warrant is retrieval-level (`in_window@10` on synthetic strata); no outcome-level evidence for F18 exists, and Q1/OUTCOME's own S-ident tasks showed retrieval deltas not converting (b=c=0 included the identifier stratum, 2304). Applied uniformly at the outcome level, the DECLEX closure was unnecessary; applied uniformly at the retrieval level, vectors retain a seed-robust unclosed prose advantage (−7.25/−7.67 pp; 92 vs 82) and the ledger's benefit side is non-empty in the admitted units. The implicit reconciliation — evidence demands scale with cost, and F18's cost is ~0 — is defensible, but the memo never states it; it should.
- **"Certain, large, permanent"** (4015; also 4003–4004 "costs are certain"). The 169 ms/470 MB figures are the *brute-force* configuration at 153k with the differentiator never enabled (406–407); arm A (IVF-PQ) has zero measurements by the memo's own table (3892). The memo does flag this honestly once ("or else an A-vs-C benchmark program whose only purpose is keeping the option alive," 3912) — partial credit — but the closing rhetoric prices vectors at their worst configuration while cancelling the benchmark that would price the best one. The 7.2 h embed is a build-time, cached, incremental cost, not a permanent per-query one. "Certain" is fair for the dependency/complexity costs; "large, permanent" for the full list is advocacy. Note also the rule's precedent claim (3902: "the same rule that eliminated arm B") is inexact — B was eliminated on cost thresholds alone, benefit never entered; it is not precedent for benefit-conversion reasoning.
- **Does the rule, honestly applied, keep vectors?** No reading I can construct keeps them *on this record*: the only measured outcome benefit is a zero point estimate (bounded per F1), the retrieval benefits sit on strata whose connection to the workload is unestablished in either direction, and kluster-anti is one-directional against vectors (HANDOFF:132–133). The deeper problem is that the rule converts under-powered measurement into zero-valuation — which is why F1's bounds must appear in the memo — but with the bounds stated, the cost-benefit still lands on D given the never-shipped, fully-reversible context (memory: no customer data; deletion needs no back-compat).

### F7 (MODERATE — C audit): Gap confrontations are genuine in structure; two are weakened by the findings above, and the memo misses one real alternative

Per-gap: **Gap 1** — genuinely confronted (three measured facts, named residual risk), but fact 2 carries F2 and the omissions of F1. **Gap 2** — the mechanical-boundedness argument is real confrontation; the two supporting stats are F3's circularity/denominator problems; "harvest monitoring" as mitigation is F4's fig leaf. **Gap 3** — honestly named "the least-evidenced leg" (3971), which is the right label; still owes the 22.1%/39% bounds, which belong exactly here. **Gap 4** — the most honest of the five ("window-parity, not exact-hit parity, and is recorded as such," 3979–3980), marred only by the "empirically dominant" over-read (F2.2).

**Gap 5 (doc chunks)** — to the memo's credit, an unmandated gap named, and the "unmeasured in either direction" symmetry (3990) is correct. One flaw: the re-entry trigger "if doc search becomes load-bearing **as a feature**" (3992) ignores that `.md` chunks are indexed and ranked by the shipped pipeline **today** (corpus table 635–644 includes `.md`; Q1/OUTCOME had to *exclude* doc chunks explicitly, 2326–2327). The exposure starts at deletion, not at a future product decision; current doc-query traffic is unmeasured (and unmeasurable — see F4's telemetry point).

**Missed:** (a) **The shipped `--no-embeddings` configuration** (1932: "`mode: "lexical"` is already a shipped, supported configuration (§13.11 `--no-embeddings`)"; 1982: the experimental L arm *was* this configuration). This cuts both ways and the memo uses neither: it is additional pro-delete evidence (lexical-only is already a supported mode, and per the 83% datum was the de facto mode for a period), and it constitutes an unweighed **middle option** — flip the default, keep the subsystem dormant — that captures the embed-time/RAM/model-layer costs with full reversibility while keeping the 91 MB dep and code complexity. The handoff's §4a offered a binary (delete/keep-architecture), so the memo followed its mandate — but a ratifier should see the third option and the reason to reject it (never-shipped ⇒ reversibility is cheap anyway; dormant dead code contradicts the deletion-hygiene rationale). (b) **Non-TS-language generalization** — the closure population is TSDoc-derived TS declarations; the corpus limit is named (3980–3981) but the language limit is not; one sentence suffices. (c) The **zero-result suggestions assist** is `identifier_fts`-based lexical (HANDOFF:71–73) — unaffected by the deletion; I checked and this is *not* a missed gap; I won't manufacture one. (d) **Mode reporting** is explicitly deferred (4031–4032) rather than waved away — acceptable, and deletion actually removes the known silent-lexical-decay defect (HANDOFF:233–234).

### F8 (LOW — rhetoric): "a one-line-of-concept lexical ranker" (4010–4011) — the shipping artifact is a symbol-gated ranker with dotted-segment matching, ordering rules, a regression suite, and a measured-harmful sibling variant. ## What survived the attack

The core structure is sound and I will not pretend otherwise: the four pre-registered lines do converge (HANDOFF §2); the DECLEX closure is real at registered scope and the memo carries all four of its caveats at full strength (Gaps 2–4 map directly onto them); the memo was written before any benchmark or deletion work, as mandated; the option-set and most numbers are faithful to the record; the anti-delete flags from HANDOFF §8 all appear in the memo rather than being buried; and the never-shipped context makes the bet cheap to reverse. No finding above reverses the direction of the evidence — they weaken the *stated* support and the *safeguards*, not the conclusion.
